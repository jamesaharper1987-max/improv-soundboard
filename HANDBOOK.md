# Improv Soundboard – handbook

James's own iPad soundboard for improv shows, built with Claude to replace a €200 paid soundboard app. This file is the reference for any new chat: what exists, where it lives, how it works, and how to change it.

Last updated: 28 October 2026 (evening).

## How James uses it on the iPad

1. Open the live link in Safari once, wait for "50 sounds on the board", then Share → Add to Home Screen.
2. Always open it from the home-screen icon. That app keeps its own storage, separate from Safari, so it downloads the sounds once on its first launch.
3. Tap **GO** before the show. It loads the short sounds into memory and keeps the screen awake.
4. After the first load it works with no internet.
5. The **☰ menu** (top right, next to Stop all) has **Edit board** (names, pictures, volume, looping and order; add or delete sounds; **Back up board** saves a small backup file; **Playback log** lists every hiccup of a long sound; **Done editing** in the top bar ends it), **Show / Hide keyboard panel**, and **Keyboard settings** (where MIDI works).

## What the app does (current features)

- **Light theme (27 Oct 2026)**: warm paper-coloured page, white tiles and panels, dark text. The tile pictures keep their dark, colour-coded backgrounds. Amber still marks a playing one-shot and teal (a deeper version of the old cyan, so it reads on white) a playing loop. Before 27 Oct the app was dark-only.
- A grid of tiles, one per sound, each with a drawn picture and a name. Tiles are grouped into seven colour-coded sections: Weather & water, Nature & night, City & travel, Places, Home & office, Bells & alarms, Stings & hits. Sounds added on the iPad go under "Added sounds".
- Tap a tile to play, tap again to stop (short fade so it doesn't click).
- **Fade out (per sound)**: while a sound plays, a **Fade out** button sits in the top-right corner of its tile. Tapping it fades only that sound away over **6 seconds**; everything else keeps playing. While fading, the tile pulses and the button reads "Fading…". Tapping the tile during a fade starts the sound fresh; Stop all cuts a fading sound at once.
- **Stop all** fades everything out over 1.5 seconds. The top bar with Stop all stays pinned at the top of the screen however far down the board is scrolled.
- Each tile has its own volume slider, which works while the sound plays and is remembered.
- Any number of sounds can play at once (layering).
- **Loop with crossfade**: a looped sound starts a fresh copy 10 seconds before the end and crossfades the two, so clips that start with silence and build up (the vacuum cleaner) still sound continuous. Clips shorter than about 22 seconds get a shorter crossfade.
- **Keyboard voices lookup**: a side panel (on the iPad it is open beside the board from the start; hide or show it from the ☰ menu or its Close button, which is remembered; on a phone it opens from the ☰ menu) listing all 820 voices of James's Yamaha PSR-EW425. Type a word ("whistle", "door", "siren") or a number and it shows the voice number to dial on the keyboard, its group and how far into the group it sits. There are no filter chips any more (removed 25 Oct to save space): typing a group name such as "organ" or "strings" also finds that group's voices, and the match count only shows while searching. Each row shows only the number and name (the star and the grey "group · MIDI" line were removed on 25 Oct at James's request; panel-only voices still say to choose them on the keyboard). Drum-kit keys can be listed too: "whistle" also shows the samba whistles in Standard Kit 1 (voice 296, keys B4 and C5). The panel sits beside the board, so tiles and Stop all keep working while it is open. In Safari (the home-screen app) it only looks numbers up.
- **Send to keyboard (where MIDI is available)**: in a browser that supports Web MIDI (Chrome on the Mac, or the iPad app **MIDIWeb Browser**), the Keyboard panel shows a **Connect keyboard** box. With the PSR-EW425 plugged in by USB (its USB TO HOST port), tapping a voice switches the keyboard to it; the sound still comes from the keyboard. The current voice is highlighted. The box also has a MIDI channel choice (default 1, remembered), a device picker when more than one MIDI device is present, and **Test note** (plays middle C for under a second). Voices marked "panel only" (layers, arpeggios) can't be sent and must be chosen on the keyboard. Tapping a drum-kit key row switches to that kit. After the first connection the app reconnects by itself each time the panel opens. (Since 25 Oct the box lives in the Keyboard settings window, see below; the footer tip is hidden while linked.)
- **Keys play the voice chosen here** (switch in the Connect box): first real test (25 Oct) showed the PSR-EW425 does **not** change its panel (Main) voice from MIDI; a program change only changes the part that plays incoming MIDI (Test note played in the new voice, the keys didn't). With this switch on, the app turns the keyboard's Local Control off (sends CC 122 = 0) and relays every key press, pedal and pitch bend coming in over USB straight back to the keyboard on the chosen channel, so the keys play the voice tapped in the panel and the sound still comes out of the keyboard. Turning the switch off (or leaving the page) turns Local Control back on. If the keyboard ignores the CC 122 message, notes sound twice and Local Control must be set to Off by hand in the keyboard's Function menu. Since 27 Oct, tapping a voice's name, L / R / Both, or a scene all turn this on by themselves.
- **Keyboard scenes** (Scenes tab in the Keyboard panel, only where MIDI works, i.e. MIDIWeb Browser or Chrome): one tap sets the PSR-EW425 up with up to three voices at once: **Right hand** (keys at or above the split point), **Left hand** (below it) and a **Whole keyboard layer**. Each part has its own octave shift (−2 to +2) and volume; each scene has a split point (− / + or **Set from keyboard**: press the key), a reverb amount and an optional **board sound** that starts with the scene (and fades out when another scene that started a different sound takes over). Since 25 Oct the starter scenes have **no** board sound (James felt they didn't fit); the option is there for scenes he makes or edits himself. **41 starter scenes** (the first ten plus 31 from James's ideas board, see "The starter scenes" below), sorted into seven genres. **Genre chips** across the top of the Scenes tab (All, Mystery & crime, Horror, Fantasy, Sci-fi, Comedy & fun, Romance & drama, Places & world, and My scenes for scenes James made himself) filter the list, each with a count; the chosen chip is remembered on that device. **Playing notes**: most starters carry a short tip (from the ideas board, e.g. "Low E pressed hard, slow then faster…") that appears on the card while that scene is on. **New scene** / **Edit**: choosing a part's voice jumps to the Voices list with an orange "Choose the … voice" bar; the voice is heard straight away. The editor also has a **Genre** choice and a **Playing notes** box. **Save and play** saves and applies. Turning on a scene switches keys-through on automatically. Tapping a single voice, or choosing a voice on the keyboard itself, ends the scene (last choice wins). Scenes are saved on that device (IndexedDB `meta` key `kbScenes`, copy in `localStorage` `kb-scenes`); they are **not** in the board backup file yet.
- **Live mix: L / R / Both on every voice** (Voices tab, only where MIDI works): each voice row has three buttons on its right. **Tap the name** (the left side of the row: number and name) and that voice becomes **the only voice on the keys, on both hands** (Both lit), from the list or from the pinned strip (James's request, 27 Oct): it ends any scene or live mix, empties the pinned strip down to that voice, silences every other MIDI channel, sends the voice with the default volume (100) and reverb (64) so nothing left over from a scene carries on, and turns keys-through on. **L** or **R** adds the voice to that hand (tap again to take it off); **Both** puts it on both hands. There is **no limit per hand** (James, 26 Oct: "I'll decide that based off my intuition"); the only ceiling is the keyboard's 16 MIDI channels (15 usable, channel 10 is skipped), and the app says so if it's reached. **Pinned voices**: every voice on the keys sits in a fixed strip above the list, in the order chosen, with its own L / R / Both buttons; turning it off removes it from the strip. It shows the single voice too (as Both) and the voices of a playing scene. The strip scrolls by itself if it gets taller than 40% of the panel. L / R / Both start from whatever is on the keys: a single voice counts as Both, and a playing scene is taken over with its voices. A **Live mix: now on the keys** box in **Keyboard settings** (⚙) lists Left and Right, the split point (− / + or **Set from keyboard**, shown when the hands differ) and **Save as scene** (opens the scene editor with the mix filled in, genre My scenes). Scenes saved this way stay on that device: they never reach GitHub, but app updates don't remove them either. Tapping a name, choosing a scene, or pressing a voice button on the keyboard ends the live mix.
- **Layer range in scenes**: the scene editor's layer part has a **Plays on** choice (Whole / Right / Left), so saved scenes can have a dual right or dual left too.
- **Top bar (25 Oct, late evening)**: just the title, **Stop all** and a **☰ menu** button; the Keyboard and Edit board buttons were removed so nothing gets tapped by mistake on stage. **Done editing** appears in the bar only while editing the board.
- **Full-height Keyboard panel (26 Oct)**: on the iPad the panel runs from the top of the screen to the bottom; the top bar (title, Stop all, ☰) shrinks to the board's width on its left, and the ☰ menu drops down under its button.
- **Endless voice list**: the Keyboard panel's voice list goes round like a wheel: scrolling past 820 brings voice 1 back, and scrolling up past 1 brings 820. A search result that is longer than the screen wraps the same way; one that fits on the screen doesn't move.
- **Now playing bar** (bottom of the screen): appears whenever something is playing and lists every playing or fading sound, wrapping into extra rows when there are several. Each item has two rows: the sound's name (tap it to scroll to its tile, which flashes), **Fade out** (6 s, as on the tile) and ■ to stop it straight away; underneath, a **volume slider** with the number beside it. The slider is the same control as the tile's slider: moving either one moves the other, changes the sound straight away (also while it is fading out) and is remembered. Loops have a teal edge, one-shots amber, fading ones a dashed edge until they disappear. The board gets extra space at the bottom so the bar never hides the last tiles, and pop-up messages sit above it.
- **Keyboard panel beside the board**: on screens 700 px wide or more, opening the Keyboard panel shrinks the board so its tiles reflow into the space on the left instead of being covered. On a phone the panel still covers the board.
- **Keyboard settings moved out of the panel**: the connection box (device, channel, Test note, "Keys play the voice chosen here") now lives in a **Keyboard settings** window, opened from the ⚙ button in the Keyboard panel, from the ☰ menu, or from Edit board. The panel itself only shows a one-line "Keyboard not linked · Connect" bar when the keyboard isn't linked; once linked that bar disappears and a small teal dot on the ☰ menu button shows the link.
- **Sound comes back by itself after an iPad pause (27 Oct 2026)**: iPadOS can pause the app's sound engine mid-show (a notification, Siri, the screen locking, the audio route changing, e.g. when the keyboard's USB cable, which also carries audio, is plugged in or out). Before, the board then went silent while the keyboard kept working, until the app was reloaded. Now, if the engine is paused for more than a moment while something is playing, a message says "The iPad paused the sound. Tap any sound to bring it back." The next tile tap builds a fresh sound engine, restarts the loops that were playing (one-shots that were cut off are not replayed) and plays the tapped sound, with the message "Sound restarted…". A frozen engine (one that says it is running but whose clock has stopped) is caught the same way.
- **Long sounds pick up again after a hiccup (28 Oct 2026)**: if a long sound (over 40 s, streamed) errors, is paused by the iPad, stops moving, or a loop's hand-over copy is refused, the app restarts it from the same spot with a short fade-in, up to four times; if streaming keeps failing it plays that sound on from memory. "Not loaded" now only means the file itself can't be read.
- **Playback log (28 Oct 2026)**: Edit board → **Playback log** lists every hiccup (time, sound, what went wrong, where in the clip) and every time the iPad paused the sound. **Copy** puts it on the clipboard to paste to Claude; **Clear** empties it. It lives on that device only.
- Works offline once installed, through a service worker.
- Pictures, names, loops and volumes can all be changed in Edit board.

## Decisions made so far

- Platform: a web app on the iPad home screen, hosted free on GitHub Pages. No App Store, no yearly fee.
- **Related sounds removed (25 Oct 2026)**: the strip of related sounds covered about a third of the screen and James found it unnecessary. The strip and its Edit board section are gone. The `links` field is still in `board.json` and stored records, but the app ignores it.
- **Fade out is per sound, not "fade all"** (James chose this). Fixed length 6 seconds (`FADE_SECS` in `index.html`), with a curve that drops gently rather than a straight ramp.
- Defaults for the spec's open questions: tap-to-stop uses a short fade; Stop all fades over 1.5 s; crossfade is fixed at 10 s for all loops.
- The six built-in example sounds were removed and replaced with James's 50 sounds.
- Tile pictures are simple drawn icons in one consistent style, not photos from the web.
- `Selection.mp3` was left out because it looked like a duplicate export of `Cicada.mp3`.
- `Car purr.mp3` is shown as **Cat purr** (its original file was a cat purring). `Incadescent light` was corrected to **Incandescent light**. Long names were shortened (e.g. "Busy nightclub" became "Nightclub").
- **Keyboard lookup is read-only (25 Oct 2026)**: James wanted to type a sound name and find the Yamaha voice. Safari on iPad has no Web MIDI, so the web app cannot switch the keyboard's voice; it shows the number to dial instead (the "poor man's version"). Switching the voice automatically over USB needs a native iPad app (Xcode, Apple developer account). The voice list file is kept so that app can reuse it.
- **Switching voices from the iPad (25 Oct 2026)**: James wants to tap a voice and have the Yamaha change. Safari and every iPad browser use Apple's engine, which has no Web MIDI; Chrome on iPad is the same engine, and no browser with its own engine had shipped on iPad in the EU by mid-2026. Chosen route: build the sending into the web app using the standard Web MIDI API, so it works in Chrome on the Mac and in MIDIWeb Browser on the iPad (a free app, 5of12 LLP, that adds Web MIDI). If MIDIWeb Browser doesn't work with USB or isn't reliable on stage, the fallback is a small native iPad app that wraps the same web app and provides MIDI (Xcode; same code).
- **Free software (25 Oct 2026)**: James intends to offer the soundboard to others for free, with an optional "buy me a coffee" link for people who like it (see "Planned next").
- Ambiences loop, short clips play once, Microwave/Printer/Security alarm/Cat purr loop. Starting volume: 60 for loops, 85 for one-shots.
- **New scenes from the ideas board (25 Oct 2026)**: James asked for more scenes inspired by his Freeform ideas board, first as recommendations, then built in. 31 scenes were added, using the voices he noted where they can be sent over MIDI. Corrections found on the board while doing this: **791 is Horse; Wind chime is 799**. These voices on the board are panel-only and can't be in a scene: **152** Trumpet & Trombone (scene uses **584** Trumpet & Trombone Section instead), **021** Tiny Electric Piano (13 or 17 instead), and the arpeggios **333** Hybrid, **336** Strum, **342** Harpeggio, **349** Club Adds (still usable when picked on the keyboard itself, which ends a scene).
- **No board sounds in the starter scenes (25 Oct 2026)**: James tried the scenes with board sounds attached (crickets, restaurant, rain…) and decided they didn't fit. All 41 starters now have `sound: null`, and the tips that mentioned board sounds were reworded. A one-time cleanup removes the sound from starter scenes already saved on a device (matched by key or name; scenes James made keep theirs, and a sound he adds to a starter afterwards stays). The board-sound choice stays in the scene editor.
- **Ghost story scenes (27 Oct 2026)**: for a 20th-century play whose characters turn out to be ghosts at the end, James asked for four themes "quite different to the ones already there". They are a set to step through in order: **Parlour** (the normal world: salon violin over a mellow piano), **Something's off** (celesta with a quiet bowed pad on the right hand, heartbeat on the bottom keys), **The reveal** (Vox Humana voices over a low tubular bell) and **Aftermath** (air bells with a soft air choir, wind on the bottom keys). None of their ten voices is used by any other starter, and the voices for the hints (heartbeat, wind) are the keyboard's own sound effects rather than board sounds. They have their own genre, **Ghost story**, and the names are numbered so they sit in order. The idea behind them: play one tune in the Parlour and bring it back slowly on the voices at the reveal, after a Stop all on the board for a moment of silence.
- **Scenes using a sound-effect zone**: Flashback / dream (811 Submarine), Someone's in the house (816 Footsteps), Ghost 2 (815 Heartbeat) and Ghost 4 (786 Wind) put a sound-effect voice on the left hand with the split lowered to MIDI note 48 (C2 in Yamaha naming), so only the bottom keys trigger the effect.
- **Live mix from the Voices list (25 Oct 2026, late evening)**: James wanted to pick a second voice while playing and put it on the left or right hand or layer it, and to have two voices per hand. A first version used one switch at the top of the list (Replace · Right · Left · Layer); James preferred buttons on each voice row, so the switch was replaced the same evening by **L / R / Both** on each row, with tap-the-name = replace. First built with two voices per hand at most (the PSR-EW425 plays 64 notes at once, so many layered voices with the sustain pedal can run out); on 26 Oct James asked to remove the cap and decide himself. Save as scene was kept after checking that scenes saved on the iPad survive app updates (they just don't go to GitHub).
- **Tap the name = only voice, Both (27 Oct 2026)**: James asked that pressing an instrument on the left side of its row override everything and make it the only track, with Both selected. Before, tapping the name ended the scene/live mix and changed the voice on the main channel, but left the volume and reverb a scene had set on that channel, didn't silence the other parts, and didn't switch keys-through on. Now it resets all of that (volume 100, reverb 64, octave 0) and turns keys-through on.
- **Volume in the Now playing bar (25 Oct 2026)**: James chose this as the quickest of the four planned features. Each Now playing item got a second row with a full-width volume slider (rather than a small pop-up), so it is easy to grab on stage.
- **Boss fight's bass (26–27 Oct 2026)**: 99 Fat Lo Bass is naturally far louder than 110 Marcato Strings. Lowered from 100 to 45 on 26 Oct (Video game's 513 Square Bass to 65 at the same time); on 27 Oct James said it was still too loud, so it went down to **35**, with a second one-time fix for saved copies (see Technical notes). All by estimate, to be checked by ear; James can fine-tune it on the iPad (Scenes → Boss fight → Edit → Left hand volume → Save and play).
- **Stream recovery and Playback log rebuilt (28 Oct 2026)**: on 25 Oct Busy restaurant dropped out and showed "Not loaded"; a chat that day rebuilt stream recovery and added a Playback log, but those files were never pushed and later pushes replaced them. James asked on 28 Oct for the changes from earlier chats to be incorporated, so both were rebuilt on the current code. Together with the engine recovery below, this covers a single long sound failing and the whole engine being paused.
- **Recovering from a paused sound engine (27 Oct 2026)**: James had the sound effects stop mid-session while the keyboard kept working; only a reload brought them back. The likely cause: iPadOS paused the Web Audio engine and every tile tap waited for `resume()`, which on the iPad can stay pending for good. Chosen fix: never wait on `resume()` for more than 0.3 s, and if the engine isn't running (or its clock is frozen) when a tile is tapped, rebuild it inside that tap, because iPadOS only lets sound start during a tap. Restarting loops rather than one-shots: a cut-off sting replayed late would land in the wrong moment.
- **Light theme (27 Oct 2026)**: James asked for light mode instead of dark. Dark was originally chosen for side-stage use with the house lights down; the light theme replaces it completely (there is no switch). If a dark option is wanted back, the colours are all in the `:root` variables, so a toggle would be a small change.

## How changes get made (workflow for new chats)

Claude cannot push to GitHub from a chat, because James's GitHub account is not linked to Claude. The working pattern is:

1. **Claude gets the current code.** The repo is public, so Claude can clone `https://github.com/jamesaharper1987-max/improv-soundboard` in its workspace. ### Important: how updates reach the iPad

- **New sounds** added to `board/board.json` (new `id`) are downloaded automatically the next time the app opens online.
- **A replaced sound file** (same sound, better recording): replace `board/audio/<name>.mp3`, update that entry's `duration` and `bytes`, and **raise its `rev`** (no `rev` means 1). Each device then downloads the new audio the next time the app opens online, keeping its own name, picture, loop and volume settings. Devices where that sound's audio was replaced in the editor are left alone. `board.json` is fetched network-first by the service worker, so this happens on the first launch after the push. First used for Bees (rev 2, 25 Oct 2026).
- **Uploading in the app stays on that device**: a sound added or replaced through Edit board is stored only in that copy's storage (each of: the home-screen app, MIDIWeb online, MIDIWeb's saved copy). It never reaches GitHub or the Mac. To change a sound everywhere, it goes into the repo as above.
- **Changed settings for an existing sound** in `board.json` (name, loop, volume, picture) do **not** overwrite what is already on the iPad, so James's own edits are never lost. For an existing sound, change it in Edit board on the iPad, or delete the tile and reload the app online so the new version is re-added. (A "sync from GitHub" button could be built if this becomes annoying.)
- **A deleted sound** stays deleted on that iPad even if it is still in `board.json`.
- **New starter scenes** (a new `key` in `SC_STARTERS`) are added to each device's scene list the first time the Scenes tab opens after the update, with a message "N new scenes added to your list". Each device remembers which starter keys it has been offered (IndexedDB `meta` key `kbScenesSeen`, copy in `localStorage` `kb-scenes-seen`), so a starter deleted on the iPad never comes back, and a starter whose name already exists in the list is skipped. **Changing an existing starter** in the code does not change it on devices that already have it (same rule as sounds): edit it on the iPad, delete it there and give the starter a new `key`, or add a one-time fix in `scLoad()` (see "Scene volume fixes" in Technical notes).
- **App code changes** (`index.html`, `sw.js`) reach the iPad on the second launch after the push, because the offline cache serves the old version first and refreshes in the background. Bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` on every code change so the cache refreshes. Current value: `soundboard-2026-10-28a`. (On 27 Oct two chats both used `-27a`: the ghost scenes went up under it, the other chat's changes were renamed and shipped as `-28a`. Check the repo's current value before choosing the next one.) **MIDIWeb's saved copy only updates when it is refreshed and re-saved online** (see Recommended stage setup); on 27 Oct James heard Boss fight still too loud although the 26 Oct fix was already on GitHub, most likely for this reason.
- **Home-screen colours** (`manifest.webmanifest` and the status-bar meta tag) are read when the app is added to the home screen; after the light theme, the Safari home-screen icon may keep a dark status bar until it is removed and added again.

## Technical notes for Claude

- **Audio engine**: Web Audio API. Clips of 40 s or less are decoded into memory at GO for instant firing. Longer clips stream from on-device storage through `<audio>` elements routed into Web Audio (`createMediaElementSource`), because decoding all the long ambiences would need about 1.5 GB of memory. A pool of 16 `<audio>` elements is unlocked inside the GO tap, since iPadOS only lets an element play later if it first played during a tap.
- **Per-voice gain chain**: copies → `out` gain (the volume slider; also used for quick stops and Stop all) → `fader` gain (used only by Fade out) → master. Keeping the fade on its own node means the slider and Stop all still work mid-fade without fighting the fade automation. Fading voices move from the `playing` map to a `fading` set until they finish.
- **Volume sliders (tile and Now playing)**: both call `applyVolume(s, pct, from)`, which sets `s.volume`, moves the `out` gain of the playing voice and of any fading voice of that sound, updates every slider marked `data-vol="<id>"` with its number, and saves `prefs` 400 ms after the last move. The Now playing bar keeps its items in a map (`npEls`) and updates them in place instead of rebuilding them, so a slider being dragged isn't destroyed when another sound starts or stops; an item is only rebuilt when its sound record is replaced (e.g. after a restore).
- **Loops**: buffered loops schedule overlapping `AudioBufferSourceNode` copies with equal-power fade curves; streamed loops hand over to a fresh element when `currentTime >= duration − X`, checked every 100 ms. X = min(10 s, 45% of the clip).
- A dynamics compressor on the master bus stops layered sounds from clipping.
- **Pinned top bar**: the bar is `position: sticky`. `body` must use `min-height: 100%`, not `height: 100%`; with a fixed body height the bar scrolls away after one screen (this was the Stop all bug fixed on 25 Oct).
- **Storage**: IndexedDB database `improv-soundboard`, stores `sounds` (records hold the audio and image as blobs) and `meta` (`seeded`, `removedBundle` = ids of folder sounds the user deleted).
- **First load**: the app fetches `board/board.json` and imports any sound whose id is not yet stored and not in `removedBundle`, with progress shown on the GO screen.
- **Backup**: a JSON file. Sounds that came from the folder board are saved as references (`bundleFile`, `bundleImage`), so the backup stays small; restoring re-downloads them.
- **Tile pictures**: 160×120 SVGs, a vertical gradient in the section colour, a faint circle, and a line icon at 2.4× scale (stroke 1.9, colour #f4efe4) centred at (80, 50). Icons are from Lucide (`lucide-static@0.460.0`, ISC licence); vacuum, lawn mower, sprinkler, bowling, prison bars, bee and the purr/crash marks were drawn by hand in the same style. Section colours (top → bottom of the gradient): weather #335f8c→#1f3c5c, nature #46703e→#2a4527, city #94562a→#5c3317, places #62458a→#3c2a57, home #2f6b6c→#1b4243, bells #8a6a22→#57420f, stings #86343f→#521d25. They stay dark in the light theme; tiles without a picture show initials in light lettering (#f4efe4) on a dark gel colour.
- **Volume and order are saved separately** (`meta` key `prefs`: `{id: {volume, order}}`), applied on load over the stored records. Moving a volume slider or reordering never rewrites a sound's record (which holds the audio file). Before 25 Oct (`-25j`) every slider move re-saved the whole record including the audio; on the iPad (MIDIWeb) that failed with "Couldn't save that change on this device" and broke the long sound that was playing ("didn't start"). Full records are still written when a sound is added, edited in the editor or restored. The stream retry also re-reads the audio file from storage before trying again.
- **Saved copies (`appFetch`)**: every fetch of a file beside the page goes through `appFetch(path)`, which tries the relative path and falls back to `LIVE_SITE + path` (`https://jamesaharper1987-max.github.io/improv-soundboard/`). The voice list is also kept in `localStorage` (`kb-voices`) for offline use. GitHub Pages allows these cross-origin requests.
- **Stream recovery (28 Oct, `-28a`; replaces the old single retry)**: first built in the 25 Oct "Busy restaurant" chat (cache `-25l` there), which never reached GitHub; rebuilt on 28 Oct on top of the current code. Every failure of a streamed copy goes through `streamFail(v, copy, why)`: an `error` event, a refused `play()` (an `AbortError` is still ignored), or the 100 ms watchdog noticing a copy that hasn't moved for 4 s while playing, 1.5 s while paused by the system, or 8 s without ever starting (only counted while the engine is running, not frozen, and the page is visible). If no other live (not handed-over) copy is carrying the sound, `recover(v, at, fadeLen)` restarts it on a fresh pool element from the same position after 0.25 / 0.8 / 2 / 4 s, re-reading the file from IndexedDB each time; `streamCopy(v, s, fadeLen, at)` seeks on `loadedmetadata`. The position is the element's `currentTime` once it has played, else the copy's intended start `copy.at`, so refused retries keep the spot. A failure of the new copy in a loop hand-over restarts from 0 and fades in over what's left of the old copy's fade-out (`handT`). `v.fails` resets once a copy has played 8 s cleanly. After four failed tries `memoryFallback(v, at)` decodes that one sound into memory (stored in `buffers`) and continues it from the same spot as a buffered voice (`bufferFrom`, then the normal `scheduleCopy` loop; `v.stream = false`); `voiceStart` then keeps using the buffer for that sound for the rest of the session. If the stored file can't be decoded, a folder sound is fetched again from the live site. Only if that fails too: "Not loaded" + toast "… stopped: its file couldn't be read." Old blob URLs are no longer revoked while an element may still be playing them (`oldURLs`, `sweepURLs()` after each `dropCopy`). Tested headless (28 Oct) on Rain: an injected error, a system pause, a refused loop hand-over and permanently refused streaming (falls back to memory from 0:09 and keeps playing), then an engine pause and rebuild.
- **Playback log (28 Oct)**: `plog(name, text)` appends a timestamped line to `localStorage` `play-log` (last 80). Stream hiccups log the sound, the reason (`errText(el, err)`: media error kind and message, or the `play()` rejection) and the position; engine pauses, freezes and rebuilds log as "Sound engine". Shown in `<dialog id="plog">` from the **Playback log** button in Edit board, with Copy (clipboard, or selects the text if the clipboard is refused) and Clear.
- **Scenes, how they play**: part channels are the chosen channel (Right hand), +1 (Left hand) and +2 (layer). Applying a scene sends All Notes Off on those three channels, then per part Bank MSB/LSB + Program Change, CC 7 volume and CC 91 reverb. The relay sends each incoming note to the matching parts (shifted by octave) and remembers where every held note went (`sc.routes`), so a note held while the scene changes still stops correctly. Pedal, pitch bend and other controllers go to every part. A program change coming from the keyboard (James pressing a voice button) ends the scene. The echo guard now compares incoming messages with everything the app sent in the last 40 ms.
- **Endless list**: `kbWrap(list, n)` at the end of `kbRender()`: if the `n` rows are taller than the list, it appends two clones of them (three copies), stores the height of one copy in `kb.wrapH` and starts at the middle copy; a passive scroll listener on `#kb-list` moves `scrollTop` by one copy when it leaves the middle half (below 0.5 or above 1.5 copies). Clicks work on the clones because they are handled on the list (`data-n`, `data-hand`). Full list = 2,460 rows. On iPad, momentum scrolling may stop briefly at the jump; to check on stage.
- **Top bar and menu**: `#more-btn` opens `<dialog id="menu">` holding `#kb-toggle` (Show/Hide keyboard panel), `#edit-open` and `#menu-kbs`; a tap on an item or outside it closes the menu. `setEdit(on)` replaces the old Edit board toggle (`#edit-toggle` is now the "Done editing" button, hidden unless editing). At 700 px or wider and with the panel open, `.kb` has `top:0` (plus the safe-area inset) and `.bar` gets the same right margin as `#board`; the menu is positioned from `#more-btn`'s rectangle each time it opens. The panel opens by itself at 700 px or wider unless `localStorage` `kb-open` is `off` (set by Close or Hide); `kbToggle(force, quiet)` skips focusing the search box when quiet, so the iPad keyboard doesn't pop up. The linked dot moved to `#more-btn`.
- **Sound engine recovery (27 Oct, shipped in `-28a`)**: `buildAudio()` creates the `AudioContext`, master gain and limiter and sets `ctx.onstatechange = audioStateChanged`. `ensureAudio()` builds it if missing and, if not running, calls `resume()` but waits at most 300 ms. Watchdog state is in `aw` (`last`, `still`, `frozen`, `warned`, `rebuilds`): a 1 s interval marks the engine `frozen` when `ctx.currentTime` hasn't moved for two checks while it says `running` and the page is visible. `audioHealthy()` = running and not frozen. `audioStateChanged()` warns (toast, at most every 8 s) if the engine is still not running 1.5 s after leaving `running` while something plays. `toggle(id)` calls `rebuildAudio()` before its first `await` when `ctx` exists and isn't healthy: it stops and forgets every playing and fading voice (`playing`, `fading` cleared), pauses and empties the `<audio>` pool and drops it (a media element source belongs to one context for good), closes the old context, builds a new one, calls `resume()` and `unlockPool(24)` inside the tap, and returns the ids of the loops that were playing; `toggle` restarts those with `voiceStart`, starts the tapped sound, and shows "Sound restarted…". AudioBuffers in `buffers` are reused (they aren't tied to a context). Scene board sounds go through `toggle` too. Tested headless (27 Oct): `ctx.suspend()` with Rain (streamed loop) and Dripping water (buffered loop) playing → warning toast; next tap → new running context, both loops back (Rain's element playing), tapped sound playing; a frozen `currentTime` → `frozen` set, next tap rebuilds; normal taps afterwards toggle as usual. Not tested on a real iPad interruption yet.
- **Tap the name (single voice, 27 Oct)**: `kbSendVoice(n)` (called from `onVoiceClick` for a row tap outside the `.kb-hand` buttons, when not picking a voice for the scene editor): checks the connection with the bank MSB, turns keys-through on if it is off (keeping the list's scroll position), clears `sc.learn`, sends All Notes Off on every channel except the chosen one (`scAllOff(new Set([ch]))`, so a note held on that channel keeps sounding), sends the voice with `scSendPart({voice:n, oct:0, vol:100, ch}, 64)` (bank, program, CC 7 = 100, CC 91 = 64), sets `sc.active = null`, empties `sc.live` (L, R, order, parts; its split is kept for the next live mix) and sets `kb.current = n`, then redraws (`scRender` if on Scenes, `liveUi`, `liveMark`). Tested headless with a fake keyboard (27 Oct): a four-voice mix (1 L, 2 R, 110 R, 218 Both) collapses to 177 on Both, channels 2–16 get All Notes Off, and keys above and below the split go only to channel 1; the same from inside a scene and from the pinned strip.
- **Live mix**: `sc.live` holds `L` and `R` (voice numbers per hand, no limit), `order` (every voice on the keys, in the order chosen; drives the pinned strip and part naming) plus `split`, `reverb`, `sound`, `from` (name of the scene it started from) and `parts`; `sc.active = 'live'` while it plays, and `scCur()` returns the playing scene or the live mix for the relay. `liveHand(hand, n)` (hand `L`, `R` or `Both`) changes it; `liveParts()` maps it onto parts: a voice on both hands becomes one whole-keyboard part (`all`, `all2`…), the others `right`, `right2`, `right3`… / `left`, `left2`… (keeping octave and volume of voices carried over from a scene); `liveSend()` sends every part and All Notes Off on channels left empty (held notes on used channels keep sounding). Parts and channels: `scZones(s)` gives each part its channel: the fixed five `SC_ALLZ` = right (chosen channel), left (+1), all (+2), right2 (+3), left2 (+4), then any other part (right3, all2…) on the next free channels, skipping channel 10; parts beyond 16 channels are dropped (`liveHand` refuses the change instead). `right*` parts play at or above the split, `left*` below, `all*` everywhere (or per its `range`). `scAllOff()` sends All Notes Off on all 16 channels. `kbHands(n)` says which hands voice n is on (live mix, playing scene or single voice); `kbOnKeys()` lists them; `kbPins()` draws `#kb-pins` above `#kb-list` (same rows and click handler). Saved scenes can therefore contain `right2` / `left2`; the scene editor only shows right, left and layer but keeps the others when saving. Layer range (`parts.all.range` = all / right / left, `scInRange()`) is set in the scene editor's "Plays on". A note-off with no route while a scene is on goes to the single-voice channel, so a note held from before can't stick. `sc.learn` holds the object whose split is being set (draft or live mix). Row buttons are `.kb-hand` (`data-hand`), lit by `liveMark()`; the box is `#kb-live` inside the Keyboard settings dialog (moved out of the panel on 25 Oct to save space), drawn by `liveUi()`; Save as scene closes the dialog.
- **Scene volume fixes**: `scLoad()` runs one-time fixes on scenes already saved on a device, each marked done with its own flag. `kbScenesVol1` / `kb-scenes-vol1` (26 Oct): Boss fight left hand (99 Fat Lo Bass) → 45 and Video game left hand (513 Square Bass) → 65, only where the volume is still 100 as first shipped. `kbScenesVol2` / `kb-scenes-vol2` (27 Oct, runs after it): Boss fight left hand → 35 where it is still 45 or 100 (a value James set himself stays). Matched by key `boss` or the name "Boss fight", so a "Boss fight (live)" scene saved from the live mix is not touched. Same pattern as the board-sound cleanup; reuse it (new flag name) for any later change to shipped starters.
- **Scenes, data**: starters live in `SC_STARTERS` in `index.html`, built with the helper `scP(voice, oct, vol)`. Each has a stable `key`, a `group` (one of `SC_GROUPS`) and usually a `tip`. `scLoad()` adds any starter whose key isn't in the device's seen list (see "How updates reach the iPad"); a device that had saved scenes before keys existed counts the first ten (`SC_FIRST`) as already seen. `scGroup(s)` gives a scene's genre: its own `group`, else the starter with the same key or name (scenes saved before groups existed), else "My scenes". The chosen genre chip is stored in `localStorage` `kb-sc-group`. Saved scene objects can now carry `key`, `group` and `tip` besides `name`, `split`, `reverb`, `sound`, `parts`. The one-time board-sound cleanup is marked done with IndexedDB `meta` key `kbScenesNoSound` (copy in `localStorage` `kb-scenes-nosound`). A starter can carry a right-hand-only or left-hand-only layer by giving its `all` part a `range` (`{ ...scP(676, 0, 55), range: 'right' }`, used by Ghost 2 and Ghost 4).
- **Keyboard voice list** (`keyboard/psr-ew425-voices.json`): `voices` is an array of `[number, group, name, MSB, LSB, program]` for voices 1–820, transcribed from Yamaha's *PSR-E473/PSR-EW425 Data List* (Voice List). Program numbers are 1–128 as Yamaha prints them; send one less over MIDI. `null` MIDI values are panel-only voices (layers and arpeggios) that can't be called up over MIDI. `kitSounds` lists drum-kit keys worth finding by name (`kit` = voice number of the kit, `note` = MIDI note; the panel shows Yamaha note names, where middle C = C3). The file is in the service worker's offline list. The panel code is the "keyboard voice lookup" block in `index.html`; starred voices are saved in `localStorage` key `kb-favs`. Sending a voice = Control Change 0 (bank MSB), Control Change 32 (bank LSB), then Program Change (pc − 1), all on the chosen channel (stored as `kb-ch`, 1–16). The chosen output id is stored as `kb-out`; `kb-midi` = `on` once access was granted, which makes the panel reconnect without a tap. The send code is the "send to keyboard" block in `index.html` and only switches on when `navigator.requestMIDIAccess` exists. Tested with a fake MIDI device (bytes for Whistle: B0 00 00, B0 20 00, C0 4E). Keys-through (`kb-thru` = on/off): all channel messages from the keyboard's MIDI input are re-sent with their channel changed to the chosen one; clock, sysex and active sensing are dropped; a message identical to one sent in the last 40 ms is treated as an echo and dropped, and over 1,500 messages a second switches the mode off, so a keyboard that echoes MIDI can't cause a loop. Leaving the page (`pagehide`) sends Local Control on. The list was read out of the PDF by machine, so if a number ever looks wrong on the keyboard, check it against the Data List and fix it in the JSON.

Amber `--live` #d97706 marks a playing one-shot (dark text `--live-ink` on amber fills; `--live-text` #a85a00 for small amber text), teal `--loop` #0b7f94 a playing loop (`--loop-t` = its RGB numbers, used as `rgba(var(--loop-t), alpha)` for tints), `--danger` #c8342a. `color-scheme: light`; `theme-color` and the manifest colours are #f3efe7; the status-bar style is `default` (was `black-translucent`, which would put white status text on the light page).
